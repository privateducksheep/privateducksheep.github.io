Case study: cutting policy look-ups from 20 minutes to 4

How the enquiries team used Answer Studio on live calls, and the content clean-up that made it work.

CA✦
MCMaya Chen
Customer Services

The enquiries team handles about 2,500 calls a week. Most are routine, but roughly one in ten involves a policy detail the officer is not sure about. Before Answer Studio, those calls meant putting the caller on hold, searching the intranet, and often messaging a team lead. We timed 50 of these look-ups in March: the median was 20 minutes, including call-backs.

The pilot

We gave Answer Studio to 12 officers for six weeks. They used it only for questions they would otherwise have looked up, and logged each one: the question, whether the answer was right, and how long it took.

Over the six weeks they logged 640 questions. Officers rated 81% of answers as correct and complete, 12% as partly right, and 7% as wrong or missing. Median look-up time fell to 4 minutes.

The wrong answers taught us the most

When we traced the 45 wrong answers, 29 came from outdated articles. The tool had cited them faithfully; the articles themselves were out of date. Another 9 came from two articles that contradicted each other. Only 7 were genuine model errors.

So the biggest fix was in the content. We sent each content owner a list of their articles that Answer Studio had cited, sorted by citation count. The 50 most-cited articles were reviewed within a month, and the two contradictory pairs were merged.

Lesson: an AI answer tool is a very effective audit of your knowledge base. Plan for content owners' time before you launch.

What officers said

Officers cared most about the citations. Several said they would not use an answer they could not check while the caller waited. They also liked that the tool says when it cannot find something, so they know to escalate straight away.

I used to dread the tricky calls. Now I can usually answer while they're still on the line.

Where it is now

Answer Studio is now used by all 140 enquiries officers. Questions marked wrong are reviewed weekly, and the rate has stayed under 5% since the content clean-up. The next step is giving content owners a monthly report of which articles are cited most, so they review those first.
