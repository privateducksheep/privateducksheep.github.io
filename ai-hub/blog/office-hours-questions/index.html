What 140 office-hours questions taught us

Six months of drop-in sessions, sorted into the five things people get stuck on most.

WH✦
HGHannah Goh
People & Culture

Every Thursday afternoon, someone from the AI team and one or two champions sit in a video call that anyone can join. Over the last six months, 96 people came at least once and asked 140 questions between them. We logged every question and sorted them afterwards.

1. "Am I allowed to?" (38 questions)

The largest group was about permission: which tools are approved, what data can go in, whether AI-drafted letters need a disclaimer. Most of these have clear answers on the guidelines page, so we now link it at the start of every session and in the tool footers.

2. "It gave me a bad answer" (31 questions)

In about two thirds of these, the prompt was missing context the person had in their head: who the audience was, what format they wanted, which policy applied. We rewrote the prompt together during the session, and it usually took five minutes.

3. "Which tool should I use?" (27 questions)

People were unsure whether a task belonged in Doc Companion, Prompt Lab or Answer Studio. We added a "Which tool?" table to the Resources page, organised by task instead of by product.

4. "How do I get my team started?" (24 questions)

Team leads wanted a short demo they could run themselves. The champions starter kit grew out of these conversations.

5. "Can it do this?" (20 questions)

Ideas for new uses, ranging from translating forms to forecasting staffing. Four of these became use cases on product pages, and one became the brief for Query Desk.

Office hours run every Thursday, 3 to 4 pm. Bring a real task. You do not need to book.

What we changed

We now open each session by asking everyone to share their task in one line, then group similar questions together. Sessions run closer to time, and people hear answers to questions they had not thought to ask. Attendance has held at 8 to 15 people a week for three months.
