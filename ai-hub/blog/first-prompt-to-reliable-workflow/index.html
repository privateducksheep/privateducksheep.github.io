From first prompt to reliable workflow

The four steps our team uses to turn a prompt that worked once into one that works on the fiftieth case.

FR✦
YOYou
Digital Products
EditMetrics

Most teams start with a prompt that produced one impressive answer in a demo. Three weeks later the same prompt is giving uneven results, and nobody can say whether it got worse or the inputs got harder. This is the process we now follow on our team before any prompt goes into daily use.

1. Collect five real examples first

Before touching the prompt, we pull five real cases from the last month: two easy ones, two typical ones and one that usually causes trouble. For each case we write two or three lines describing what a good answer must contain. That list becomes the test set. It takes about 40 minutes, and it is the step people most want to skip.

2. Write the prompt in four parts

Our prompts follow the same shape every time:

  1. Role and audience. "You are drafting a reply for a member of the public who has asked about…"
  2. Context. The policy extract or case notes, pasted in full.
  3. Task and format. "Write three short paragraphs. Use the customer's name once. End with the next step."
  4. Limits. "If the extract does not answer the question, say so and stop."

The limits section does the most work. Without it, the model fills gaps with confident guesses.

3. Change one thing at a time

When an answer misses, the temptation is to rewrite the whole prompt. We change one instruction, rerun all five examples and score each one from 0 to 2 against the checklist. A change that fixes one case and breaks another shows up immediately. We log every version in Prompt Lab with its score, so we can roll back.

Try this: keep the scoring sheet next to the prompt. Version, date, score per example, total. Ten minutes per change.

4. Name the reviewer

A workflow is only reliable when someone owns the check. We write the reviewer's role into the template itself ("Reviewed by: duty team lead") and agree what they look for: facts against the source, tone, and the next step. On our enquiries workflow the review takes about two minutes per reply.

What changed for us

Our acknowledgement prompt went through nine versions in two weeks. The score on the five examples went from 6 out of 10 to 9 out of 10, and the one case that still fails is a genuinely ambiguous policy question that goes to a person anyway. We now run the test set again whenever the underlying policy changes.
